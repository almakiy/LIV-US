Principles tell you what good governance looks like. They do not tell you what to write on Monday morning. This framework is LIV Editorial's way of organizing the work. It draws on ISO 37000:2021 [1], the G20/OECD Principles [2], the COSO internal control framework [3], the IIA Three Lines Model [4] and ISO 31000:2018 [5]. It is not a standard, and an organization can arrange the same content differently. Its job is to make sure nothing essential is missing.

## Layer 1: Purpose and accountability

**What it settles:** why the organization exists, who owns it, who governs it and to whom each of them answers.

- **Documents:** purpose and values statement; ownership register; governance charter, or the articles of association for a company; the governing body's terms of reference.
- **Evidence it works:** decisions refer back to the purpose. Owners, directors and managers can each say what they are accountable for.

ISO 37000 places purpose first among its principles, with accountability as a foundation that every other arrangement relies on [1].

## Layer 2: Decision rights

**What it settles:** which decisions the owners keep, which the governing body keeps, and which are delegated, to whom, and within what limits.

- **Documents:** reserved matters list; delegation of authority matrix; committee terms of reference; signing and payment authorities.
- **Evidence it works:** every significant decision can be traced to someone who had the authority to take it. Exceptions are recorded and reported.

A delegation of authority is where governance becomes visible in daily work. A separate template in this collection gives a starting matrix.

## Layer 3: Oversight and control

**What it settles:** how the governing body knows that strategy, risk, conduct and performance are on track.

- **Documents:** risk management policy and risk register; internal control framework; compliance obligations register; internal audit or independent review plan.
- **Evidence it works:** risks are reviewed on a set cycle. Control failures are found and fixed. The governing body receives independent assurance, not only management's own view.

Three references fit together here:

| Reference | What it contributes |
|---|---|
| ISO 31000 | Principles, framework and process for managing risk [5] |
| COSO Internal Control (2013) | Five components: control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring activities [3] |
| IIA Three Lines Model | Roles: the governing body; management (first and second line roles); independent internal audit (third line) [4] |

## Layer 4: Transparency and stakeholder engagement

**What it settles:** what is disclosed, to whom, when, and how concerns reach the people who must act on them.

- **Documents:** reporting calendar; disclosure policy; conflict of interest and related party policy; speak-up (whistleblowing) policy; stakeholder map.
- **Evidence it works:** owners and stakeholders receive accurate information on time. Conflicts are declared before decisions, not after. People who raise concerns are protected.

The G20/OECD Principles treat disclosure and transparency as a pillar of their own [2]. ISO 37000 includes stakeholder engagement among its enabling principles [1].

## Layer 5: Review and renewal

**What it settles:** how governance arrangements are evaluated and improved as the organization changes.

- **Documents:** annual governance review; board or owner evaluation; governance indicators; action log.
- **Evidence it works:** the framework changes when the organization grows, enters a new market, adds owners or suffers a failure.

## Using the framework

1. **Start with an honest inventory.** For each layer, list what exists today, even if it is informal.
2. **Close the biggest gap first.** For most small and growing organizations this is Layer 2, because unclear decision rights cause most disputes and most losses.
3. **Write only what you will use.** A two-page delegation of authority that people follow is worth more than a forty-page manual that nobody reads.
4. **Assign an owner to every document** and a date for its next review.
5. **Test it on real cases.** Take three recent decisions and check whether the framework would have said who could take them, who had to be told, and what record had to exist.

## Proportionality

The five layers apply to a single shop and to a group of companies alike. Only the scale changes.

| Layer | Owner-managed shop | Mid-size company | Listed or regulated company |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Purpose and accountability | One-page statement; owners' agreement | Charter; board terms of reference | Articles, charter, code compliance statement |
| 2 Decision rights | Spending and discount limits | Full delegation of authority | Delegation of authority plus committee mandates |
| 3 Oversight and control | Monthly owner review; cash and stock checks | Risk register; internal control; external review | Risk committee, internal audit, external audit |
| 4 Transparency | Simple monthly report to all owners | Board pack; conflict register | Public disclosures under the listing rules |
| 5 Review | Annual half-day review | Annual review and evaluation | Annual evaluation, external review on a cycle |
