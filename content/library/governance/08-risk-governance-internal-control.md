A governing body cannot check every transaction. It relies on three things: a way of managing risk, a system of internal control, and independent assurance that both work. Four widely used references describe these pieces. They overlap, and they are designed to be used together.

## ISO 31000:2018: managing risk

ISO 31000 gives generic guidelines for managing risk in any organization [1]. It is guidance, not a requirements standard. It is built in three parts:

- **Principles:** risk management should be integrated, structured and comprehensive, customized, inclusive and dynamic. It should use the best available information, take human and cultural factors into account, and improve continually [1].
- **Framework:** leadership and commitment, with integration, design, implementation, evaluation and improvement of the arrangements [1].
- **Process:** establish the scope, context and criteria; assess risk (identify, analyze, evaluate); treat risk; then monitor, review, record and report throughout [1].

A third edition is in development at ISO. At the time of writing it was at committee draft stage [7]. Until it is published, the 2018 edition remains current.

## COSO Internal Control (2013): the five components

The COSO Internal Control Integrated Framework, updated in 2013, organizes internal control into five components [2]:

| Component | In plain words |
|---|---|
| Control environment | Integrity, ethical values, board oversight, structure, competence and accountability |
| Risk assessment | Identifying and analyzing risks to objectives, including fraud risk and change |
| Control activities | Approvals, reconciliations, segregation of duties, system access controls |
| Information and communication | Getting reliable information to the people who need it |
| Monitoring activities | Ongoing and separate evaluations, and reporting deficiencies |

The framework sets out 17 principles across these five components [2].

## COSO Enterprise Risk Management (2017)

COSO's enterprise risk management framework links risk to strategy and performance. It has five components: governance and culture; strategy and objective-setting; performance; review and revision; and information, communication and reporting [3].

## The IIA Three Lines Model (2020)

The Institute of Internal Auditors' Three Lines Model describes roles rather than rigid layers [4]:

- **The governing body** is accountable to stakeholders for oversight.
- **Management** delivers objectives. *First line roles* manage risk directly in operations. *Second line roles* provide expertise, support and challenge on risk, for example compliance, quality and health and safety functions.
- **Internal audit** provides independent, objective assurance and advice as the *third line*.

The 2020 update moved away from a purely defensive view. It stresses that the lines work together to create and protect value, and it keeps internal audit independent from management [4].

## Global Internal Audit Standards

The IIA's Global Internal Audit Standards were released in January 2024 and became effective in January 2025 [5]. They set expectations for internal audit functions, including their relationship with the board.

## Board oversight in national codes

National codes turn these ideas into duties for the companies they cover. The UK Corporate Governance Code 2024 expects boards to monitor the risk management and internal control framework and to review its effectiveness at least annually [6]. From financial years beginning on or after January 1, 2026, Provision 29 also asks for a declaration on the effectiveness of material controls [6]. The Gulf codes contain comparable duties for listed companies. They are mapped in a separate item in this collection.

## A proportionate version for small organizations

| Element | Minimum practice |
|---|---|
| Risk | A one-page register of the top ten risks, reviewed monthly by the owner |
| Control environment | A code of conduct and clear authority limits |
| Control activities | Daily cash counts, two-person payment approval, weekly review of refunds and voids, restricted system access |
| Information | One monthly report with the same headings every time |
| Monitoring | The owner's own checks, plus an annual review by an outside accountant |
| Assurance | An external reviewer or auditor once a year, even if the law does not require it |
