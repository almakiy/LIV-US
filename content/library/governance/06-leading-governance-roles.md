Governance is led by people, not documents. This briefing sets out the roles that international references assign to each party. It ends with a checklist that any governing body, from a family board to the board of a listed company, can use to test itself.

## Owners

Owners provide capital and carry the residual risk. In companies they act through the shareholders' meeting: they appoint and remove the governing body, approve the accounts and decide on fundamental changes. The G20/OECD Principles give shareholders' rights and their equitable treatment a chapter of their own [2]. That includes minority shareholders, whose protection matters most where ownership is concentrated.

## The governing body

The governing body is the board of directors, a board of trustees, an owners' council or, in the smallest business, the owner acting in that role. ISO 37000:2021 describes it as the group that directs, oversees and holds the organization accountable for fulfilling its purpose [1]. The G20/OECD Principles list its key functions [2]. Among them:

- Reviewing and guiding strategy, major plans, risk policy, budgets and business plans
- Monitoring the effectiveness of governance practices and making changes as needed
- Selecting, compensating, monitoring and, when necessary, replacing key executives, and overseeing succession planning
- Overseeing the integrity of accounting and financial reporting, including independent audit and systems of risk management and control
- Overseeing disclosure and communication

## The chair

The chair leads the governing body. The chair sets the agenda, makes sure members receive accurate and timely information, encourages open challenge and leads the evaluation of the board. The UK Corporate Governance Code 2024 separates the roles of chair and chief executive, and it expects the chair to be independent on appointment [3]. Many national codes follow the same logic, while allowing local variations.

## Independent members

Independent members bring judgment that is not tied to management or to a controlling owner. The value of independence depends on behavior, not only on the label. A recent mixed-methods study of Saudi listed companies found that the period after the governance reforms was associated with greater accountability, board independence and stronger committees. Interviews in the same study showed that formal independence did not always lead to real scrutiny, especially where ownership was concentrated [4]. The study cannot prove that the regulation caused these effects [4].

## Committees

Committees allow focused work by members with the right expertise. Their decisions remain the responsibility of the whole board unless authority has been formally delegated. The UK Code expects audit, nomination and remuneration committees in the companies it covers [3]. Many organizations add a risk committee, and some add a sustainability or a health and safety committee.

## The chief executive and management

Management runs the organization within the strategy, policies and authority delegated by the governing body. It reports openly on performance, risk and conduct, including bad news. A good chief executive treats the board's challenge as a resource, not a threat.

## Owner-managed businesses

Where the owner is also the manager, the same roles still exist. They are held by one person, or by a family. A literature review of governance in small and medium enterprises found that such firms often rely on informal mechanisms: advisory boards, outsourced directors, accountants, lawyers and finance providers in place of a formal board [5]. A proportionate approach:

- Hold a formal monthly or quarterly review, separate from daily operations
- Invite one or two experienced outsiders to an advisory board
- Keep minutes of significant decisions
- Ask an external accountant to review the accounts and controls each year

## Board effectiveness checklist

| Question | Yes / Partly / No |
|---|---|
| Can every member state the organization's purpose and strategy in two sentences? | |
| Does the board receive papers early enough to read them properly? | |
| Do papers include risks, exceptions and bad news, not only results? | |
| Is there a written list of decisions reserved for the board? | |
| Are conflicts of interest declared at the start of each meeting and recorded? | |
| Does at least one member bring independent judgment? | |
| Does the board meet without management at least once a year? | |
| Is there a succession plan for the chief executive and for the chair? | |
| Has the board evaluated its own effectiveness in the past year? | |
| Are actions from previous meetings tracked to completion? | |
