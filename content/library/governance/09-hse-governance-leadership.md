When a serious accident happens, the investigation rarely stops at the person who was hurt or the supervisor on shift. It asks what the leaders knew, what they decided, what they funded and what they checked. HSE governance is the part of governance that answers those questions *before* anyone is hurt.

## A fundamental principle

In June 2022 the ILO's member states, meeting at their annual conference, added "a safe and healthy working environment" to the ILO's fundamental principles and rights at work [1]. As a result, two conventions are now counted among the fundamental conventions [1]:

- **The Occupational Safety and Health Convention, 1981 (No. 155)** concerns national OSH policy and the duties of employers. One example is the employer's duty to ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, that workplaces, machinery, equipment and processes under their control are safe and without risk to health [2].
- **The Promotional Framework for Occupational Safety and Health Convention, 2006 (No. 187)** concerns a national policy, a national system and a national program for continual improvement of OSH [3].

These conventions are addressed to states, which turn them into national law. Employers' binding duties come from the law of the country where they operate. That law should always be checked in its official text.

## The management system view

The ILO's guidelines on OSH management systems (ILO-OSH 2001) are voluntary and are not used for certification. They describe a cycle of policy, organizing, planning and implementation, evaluation, and action for improvement. Worker participation is an essential element of the policy [4].

ISO 45001:2018 sets requirements for an OH&S management system [5]. Clause 5, *Leadership and worker participation*, covers four subjects:

| Clause | Subject |
|---|---|
| 5.1 | Leadership and commitment |
| 5.2 | OH&S policy |
| 5.3 | Organizational roles, responsibilities and authorities |
| 5.4 | Consultation and participation of workers |

ISO 45001:2018 was amended in 2024 to add climate change considerations. A second edition is in development [5]. Psychological health and safety is addressed by ISO 45003:2021, which gives guidelines for managing psychosocial risks within an OH&S management system [6].

## Three essential principles for leaders

Guidance from the UK Health and Safety Executive, written for directors, board members and business owners, sets out three essential principles [7]:

1. **Strong and active leadership from the top:** visible, active commitment from the board, and effective downward communication systems and management structures.
2. **Worker involvement:** engaging the workforce in promoting and achieving safe and healthy conditions.
3. **Assessment and review:** identifying and managing health and safety risks, accessing competent advice, and monitoring, reporting and reviewing performance.

## What the evidence says

A 2026 rapid evidence assessment, prepared to inform international standard development, reviewed 80 studies on senior leadership and OSH [8]. It found that senior leaders influence OSH outcomes through four main routes [8]:

- **Strategic direction:** policy, objectives and safety built into strategy
- **Visible leadership:** site visits and participation in safety meetings
- **Systematic oversight:** indicators, reviews, investigations and incentives
- **Cultural leadership:** a "just culture" in which people feel safe reporting problems

Most of the evidence is correlational. Small enterprises are under-represented [8]. A full digest of the assessment is available in this collection.

## Board and owner actions

- **Name the accountable leader:** one member of the governing body, or the owner, takes the lead for HSE. The whole body remains responsible.
- **Put HSE first on the agenda,** not last, at every meeting.
- **Ask for the right information:** serious incidents and near misses, open actions from investigations, results of inspections and audits, and worker concerns.
- **Fund the controls:** a risk the leadership knows about but will not fund is a governance decision, and it should be recorded as one.
- **Go and see:** leaders visit operations, talk to workers and contractors, and follow up what they hear.
- **Check competence:** people in hazardous roles, and those who supervise them, are trained and assessed before they work unsupervised.
- **Include contractors:** the organization's governance covers contractors working under its control, not only its own employees.
- **Review the system:** at least once a year, review how HSE is governed and whether it works.

## Leading and lagging indicators

| Lagging (what already happened) | Leading (what prevents harm) |
|---|---|
| Fatalities and serious injuries | Percentage of planned inspections completed |
| Lost-time injuries | Investigation actions closed on time |
| Occupational illness cases | Workers trained and assessed for high-risk tasks |
| Property damage and environmental releases | Near misses reported (more reporting is often a healthy sign) |
| Regulatory notices and fines | Leadership site visits made and issues followed up |

Lagging indicators alone tell a board about the past. A balanced set of leading indicators shows whether the controls that prevent harm are in place and working.

## Questions every governing body should be able to answer

1. What are our three most serious HSE risks, and how do we know the controls work?
2. When did we last discuss a serious incident or near miss, and what did we change?
3. How do workers raise safety concerns, and what happened to the last few concerns raised?
4. Which legal HSE requirements apply to us, and who checks that we meet them?
5. What would we say if an investigator asked us what we did to prevent this?
