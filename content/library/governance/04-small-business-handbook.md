Governance is not a privilege of large companies. A grocery, a pharmacy, a café, a car workshop or a chain of five branches has owners, money, stock, staff, customers and risks. It therefore has governance, whether anyone has written it down or not. ISO 37000:2021 is explicit that its guidance applies to organizations of every type and size [1]. This handbook translates governance into the daily reality of a small business.

## How to use this handbook

Read it once, then work through the chapters in order. Each chapter ends with **"Do this month"**: one or two actions small enough to finish. After twelve months you will have a complete, proportionate governance framework without hiring anyone.

## Chapter 1: Wear one hat at a time

In a small business the same person is often the **owner** (who invests and carries the risk), the **director** (who sets direction and checks results) and the **manager** (who runs the day). Problems start when the hats blur. Money moves between the owner's pocket and the till, decisions are taken in a hurry and never checked, and nobody steps back to look at the whole business.

- **As owner** you decide what the business is for, how much risk you accept and what return you expect.
- **As director** you set the rules, delegate authority and review results on a fixed day each month.
- **As manager** you run operations within those rules.

**Do this month:** write one page with the business's purpose, its owners and their shares, and the decisions only the owners may take.

## Chapter 2: Decide who decides

Write down the authority of every role that handles money or commitments. A simple table is enough.

| Decision | Cashier | Shift or branch manager | General manager | Owner(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Refund or exchange | Up to a small set amount | Up to a set amount | Above that | |
| Discount on the till | Fixed promotions only | Up to a set percentage | Up to a higher percentage | Above that |
| Purchase from an approved supplier | | Up to a set amount | Up to a higher amount | Above that |
| New supplier | | Proposes | Approves | Informed |
| Hiring | | Proposes | Approves within budget | Approves above budget |
| Lease, loan or guarantee | | | Proposes | Approves |

Put the actual amounts in one place and review them once a year.

**Do this month:** fill in the table with real amounts and give a copy to every role named in it.

## Chapter 3: Control the money

The COSO framework lists *control activities* as one of the five components of internal control [2]. In a shop most of them concern cash and payments.

- **Separate the business and the owner.** One business bank account. The owner is paid by salary or dividend, never from the till.
- **Count, record, compare.** Count the cash at every shift change and at closing. Record the count and compare it with the till report. Investigate any difference above a small set threshold.
- **Two people for money leaving the business.** One person prepares a payment, another approves it. Where there are only two people, the owner approves and reviews the bank statement.
- **Reconcile weekly.** Compare card terminal settlements, delivery-app payouts and bank deposits with sales.
- **Protect the gaps.** Voids, refunds, "no sale" openings of the drawer and manual price changes are the usual routes for loss. Review a report of them every week.

When there are too few people to separate duties, use **compensating controls**: the owner reviews exceptions, makes surprise cash counts and reads every bank statement.

**Do this month:** start a daily cash count sheet and a weekly review of voids and refunds.

## Chapter 4: Control the stock

- Keep a list of high-value and high-risk items and count them weekly.
- Count all stock at least once a year, and every quarter if losses are a concern.
- Match deliveries with purchase orders and invoices before payment.
- Track expiry dates for food, medicines and cosmetics, and record what is written off and why.
- Restrict who may adjust stock quantities in the system, and review adjustments.

**Do this month:** choose the twenty items most likely to go missing and start a weekly count.

## Chapter 5: Conflicts, gifts and family

Conflicts of interest are normal. Hidden conflicts are the problem.

- Anyone who decides on purchases, hiring or prices declares, in writing, any personal or family interest in a supplier, customer or competitor.
- Gifts from suppliers above a small value are declared and recorded, and cash gifts are refused.
- Relatives may work in the business, but under the same job descriptions, pay rules and reviews as everyone else. The IFC handbook on family business governance recommends written policies for family employment. Its reason is that informal arrangements are a frequent source of conflict [5].

**Do this month:** prepare a one-page declaration form and ask every decision-maker to complete it.

## Chapter 6: Know your obligations

Build a **compliance calendar**: a list of every registration, license, permit, inspection, filing and renewal, with its date and the person responsible. Typical entries include:

- Commercial registration and its renewal
- Municipal or activity licenses and signage permits
- Fire and civil defense approvals
- Health permits for food handling and staff health certificates, where they apply
- Tax registrations and periodic filings
- Social insurance and payroll obligations for employees
- Insurance policies and their renewal dates
- Lease renewal and notice dates

The exact list depends on your country, city and activity. Confirm it with the issuing authorities' official websites, or with your accountant or lawyer. Never rely on a list copied from another business.

**Do this month:** list every license and renewal date you can find in your files and put them in one calendar with reminders.

## Chapter 7: A risk register on one page

ISO 31000:2018 describes risk management as a continual process: identify, analyze, evaluate and treat risks, then monitor and review them [3]. For a small business, one page is enough.

| Risk | What could happen | Likelihood (1 to 5) | Impact (1 to 5) | What we do about it | Owner | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash theft | Loss, staff disputes | | | Daily counts, exception review | Owner | Monthly |
| Key supplier fails | Empty shelves | | | Second supplier approved | GM | Quarterly |
| Fire | Closure, injury | | | Extinguishers checked, exits clear, training | Branch manager | Monthly |
| Data loss | Customer and sales records lost | | | Backup off site | GM | Monthly |
| Loss of a key person | Operations stop | | | Written procedures, cross-training | Owner | Twice a year |

Keep the ten risks that matter most. Review them on the same day each month.

**Do this month:** score your top ten risks and name an owner for each.

## Chapter 8: People and safety

- Write a short description of every job, including the authority from Chapter 2.
- Train new staff before they handle cash, food, chemicals or machinery, and record the training.
- Make health and safety part of the owner's monthly review, not only a poster on the wall. Guidance for business owners and directors from the UK Health and Safety Executive sets out three essential principles: strong and active leadership from the top, worker involvement, and assessment and review [6].

**Do this month:** walk the premises with one employee and list what could hurt someone, then fix the top three items.

## Chapter 9: Branches and managers you cannot watch every day

When a business opens a second location, the owner can no longer see everything. Governance replaces presence.

- **A branch manager's charter:** one page with their authority, the reports they send and the rules they may not change.
- **The same daily numbers from every branch:** sales, cash difference, refunds, voids, stock losses, staff hours.
- **Visits with a checklist:** announced and unannounced, covering cash, stock, safety, customer complaints and records.
- **Rotation and holidays:** a manager who never takes leave may be hiding something, or may simply be a single point of failure.
- **Comparisons:** a branch whose refunds or losses differ sharply from the others deserves a closer look.

**Do this month:** agree with each branch manager the daily report they will send, and start comparing branches.

## Chapter 10: Let people speak up

Employees often see problems first. ISO 37002:2021 sets out guidelines for receiving, assessing, addressing and concluding reports of wrongdoing, based on the principles of trust, impartiality and protection [4]. A small business needs only the essentials:

- A way to raise a concern with someone other than the direct manager, such as the owner's phone number or email
- A promise, kept, that nobody is punished for raising a concern in good faith
- A record of each concern and of what was done

**Do this month:** tell every employee, in writing, how to raise a concern and that retaliation will not be tolerated.

## Chapter 11: The monthly governance hour

Set one hour on the same day each month. Sit away from the counter. If there are several owners, all of them attend. Use a fixed agenda:

1. Results against budget: sales, margin, costs, cash
2. Exceptions: cash differences, refunds, voids, stock losses
3. Risks: changes to the top ten
4. Compliance calendar: what is due in the next 90 days
5. People and safety: incidents, near misses, training
6. Concerns raised and complaints received
7. Decisions taken, with a short written record

Keep the record of decisions. It is your governance history, and it protects every owner.

## Chapter 12: As the business grows

| Sign | Governance step |
|---|---|
| More than one owner | A written owners' agreement on decisions, exits and disputes |
| More than one branch | Branch manager charters and daily reports |
| A loan or investor | Monthly management accounts and an annual external review or audit |
| A second generation joins | A family employment policy and, later, a family charter |
| Complex decisions | An advisory board with one or two experienced outsiders |
| Rapid growth | A full delegation of authority and an internal review of controls |
