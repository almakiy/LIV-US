Governance sets direction and oversees results. Compliance makes sure the organization keeps its legal and ethical commitments while it pursues them. ISO's committee on governance of organizations (ISO/TC 309) has produced a family of standards that work together. ISO 37000:2021 sits at the top, as guidance for governing bodies [6]. This explainer covers the four standards that put integrity into daily practice.

## Two kinds of standard

The family mixes two kinds of document, and the difference matters:

| Standard | Type | Can an organization be audited against it by a third party? |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 37301:2021, compliance management systems | Requirements with guidance | Yes |
| ISO 37001:2025, anti-bribery management systems | Requirements with guidance | Yes |
| ISO 37002:2021, whistleblowing management systems | Guidelines | No, it gives guidance only |
| ISO/TS 37008:2023, internal investigations | Technical specification (guidance) | No |

A requirements standard uses "shall" statements that an auditor can check. A guidance standard describes good practice that an organization adapts to its needs.

## ISO 37301:2021: compliance management systems

ISO 37301 sets requirements for establishing, developing, implementing, evaluating, maintaining and improving a compliance management system [1]. It follows the common management system structure used by other ISO standards, from context and leadership through to improvement. Its key ideas include:

- **Compliance obligations:** what the organization must comply with and what it chooses to commit to
- **Compliance risk assessment:** where breaches are most likely and most harmful
- **Leadership and culture:** the governing body and top management show commitment, and compliance becomes part of how the organization works
- **A compliance function** with the authority, independence and resources to do its job
- **Raising concerns and investigating them**
- **Monitoring, evaluation and improvement**

In 2024, ISO published an amendment that adds climate change considerations to the clauses on the organization's context and interested parties [2]. Several ISO management system standards received the same change.

## ISO 37001:2025: anti-bribery management systems

The second edition of ISO 37001 was published in 2025 and replaces the first edition and its 2024 amendment [3]. It sets requirements and guidance to help an organization prevent, detect and respond to bribery, and to comply with anti-bribery laws and its own commitments [3]. It covers bribery in the public, private and not-for-profit sectors, by and of the organization, its personnel and its business associates, directly or indirectly [3]. Typical elements include:

- An anti-bribery policy approved by the governing body
- A bribery risk assessment
- An anti-bribery compliance function
- Due diligence on higher-risk transactions, projects and business associates
- Financial and non-financial controls: gifts, hospitality, donations, sponsorships, facilitation payments
- Training and communication
- Raising concerns, investigation and corrective action

ISO 37001:2025 addresses bribery only. Fraud, cartels, money laundering and other offenses fall under other laws and controls, although an organization can manage them within one integrated compliance system.

## ISO 37002:2021: whistleblowing management systems

ISO 37002 gives guidelines for setting up, implementing, maintaining and improving a whistleblowing management system [4]. It rests on three principles: **trust, impartiality and protection** [4]. It describes four steps in the handling of a report:

1. **Receiving** reports of wrongdoing
2. **Assessing** them
3. **Addressing** them
4. **Concluding** the case

The protection of the person who reports, and of anyone else involved, runs through all four steps.

## ISO/TS 37008:2023: internal investigations

When a concern needs investigation, ISO/TS 37008 gives guidance on conducting internal investigations [5]. It rests on principles such as independence, confidentiality, competence, objectivity and acting lawfully. It covers planning, evidence, interviews, reporting and remediation.

## How they fit together

1. **ISO 37000** tells the governing body what to oversee [6].
2. **ISO 37301** builds the overall compliance system [1].
3. **ISO 37001** adds specific anti-bribery controls [3].
4. **ISO 37002** makes sure concerns reach the right people safely [4].
5. **ISO/TS 37008** guides the response when a concern needs investigation [5].

## Where a small organization should start

A small business does not need a certified management system to benefit from these ideas. A proportionate first step:

- Write a one-page code of conduct covering bribery, gifts, conflicts of interest and fair dealing.
- Keep an obligations register (see the drafting guide in this collection).
- Set gift and hospitality limits and keep a register.
- Check new suppliers and agents before contracting with them.
- Give every employee a way to raise a concern outside their line manager, with a promise of no retaliation.
- Record every concern and the response.
