Each GCC state has its own company law and, for listed companies, its own governance code issued by the capital market regulator. This map gives the official instruments, whom they apply to and their headline requirements. It is a starting point for reading the official texts, not a substitute for them.

> **How to use this map.** Requirements change. Several instruments below were amended in 2025 and 2026. Before relying on any point, open the official text through the link, check its date and amendments, and take legal advice. Where an official English text is a translation, the Arabic text prevails.

## Saudi Arabia

**Companies Law.** Issued by Royal Decree No. M/132 dated 1/12/1443H. It applies to all companies [1]. Among its provisions [1]:

- Partners or shareholders may conclude a **family charter** covering family ownership, governance and management, employment of family members, dividends, disposal of shares and dispute settlement. It is binding and may form part of the company's articles, provided it does not conflict with the law or the articles (Article 11).
- Managers and board members owe a **duty of care and a duty of loyalty**, including disclosing any direct or indirect interest in company transactions (Article 26).
- Without authorization, they may not have an interest in company contracts, compete with the company or use its assets, information or opportunities (Article 27).
- A board member must disclose an interest in the company's business and contracts and may not vote on it (Article 71).

The Ministry of Commerce issued the **Implementing Regulations** by Ministerial Decision No. 284 dated 23/06/1444H. They detail the duties of care and loyalty and the approval of related party dealings for unlisted joint stock companies [1]. The Capital Market Authority (CMA) issues the separate implementing regulation for listed joint stock companies, most recently amended in 2026 [3].

**Family companies.** In 2018 the then Ministry of Commerce and Investment issued a Guiding Charter for Saudi Family Companies as voluntary guidance [4]. Since the 2022 Companies Law, a family charter under Article 11 can be made binding [1].

**Corporate Governance Regulations (CMA).** Issued by CMA Board Resolution 8-16-2017 and amended by Resolution 8-5-2023 to align with the new Companies Law [2]. Headline requirements for listed companies:

- The majority of board members are non-executive directors, and independent directors number at least two or one-third of the board, whichever is greater [2].
- The chair may not hold any executive position in the company at the same time, including managing director, chief executive or general manager [2].
- The audit committee has three to five members, no executive directors, at least one member specialized in finance and accounting, and an independent director as its chair [2].
- The regulations also address conflicts of interest, related party dealings, internal audit, the board report and remuneration disclosure [2].

The CMA's English text states that Arabic is the official language of the regulations [2]. Some provisions are mandatory for the Main Market and guidance for the Parallel Market (Nomu), so check how each article applies to your company.

**Financial institutions and insurers.** The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) has issued Key Principles of Governance in Financial Institutions under its supervision [5]. Insurance supervision moved to the **Insurance Authority**, established by Council of Ministers Resolution No. 85 of 1445H [6]. The Insurance Corporate Governance Regulation, first issued in 2015, is published by the Insurance Authority [7].

**Occupational safety and health.** Articles 121 to 126 of the Labor Law set the employer's core duties: a healthy workplace, precautions against work hazards, informing and equipping workers, fire prevention and liability to others on site [8]. The Occupational Safety and Health Management Regulation, issued under Article 121, applies to establishments employing fifty or more full-time workers [9]. In March 2024 the Council of Ministers approved the Kingdom's accession to ILO Convention No. 187 [10].

**Data protection.** The Personal Data Protection Law and its implementing regulations are published by the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) [11].

## United Arab Emirates

**Regulator.** Federal Decree-Law No. (32) of 2025 replaced the Securities and Commodities Authority with the **Capital Market Authority** as its legal successor, from January 1, 2026. Earlier decisions of the Authority remain in force unless they conflict with the new law [12].

**Commercial Companies Law.** Federal Decree-Law No. (32) of 2021, in force since January 2, 2022, applies to commercial companies, with separate regimes for free zones that have their own company laws [13][14]. For joint stock companies [14]:

- A board of three to eleven members, with terms of up to three years (Article 143).
- A director with a conflicting interest must notify the board, have it recorded in the minutes and not vote (Article 150).
- Related party transactions up to 5% of capital need board approval, and above 5% general assembly approval after a valuation (Article 152).
- A joint stock company may not lend to its directors or their close relatives, except financial institutions supervised by the Central Bank (Article 153).

The Ministry also lists the amending Decree-Law No. (20) of 2025 [13].

**Private joint stock companies.** Ministerial Decision No. 137/2024 requires at least one-third of the board to be independent members, with a majority of non-executive members [15].

**Public joint stock companies.** The Governance Guide approved by Decision No. (3/Chairman) of 2020 is published by the Authority, on its corporate governance page, as a consolidated text with its amendments of 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025 [16][17]. Among its requirements [17]:

- At least one-third of board members are independent, and the majority are non-executive (Article 9, clause 5).
- At least one woman sits on the board, and this is disclosed in the annual governance report (Article 9, clause 3).
- The chair may not hold any other executive position. Since Decision No. (24/Chairman) of 2025, effective August 30, 2025, the chair may also be the company manager only if all of these conditions are met: the articles permit it, at least three-quarters of the board and every member of the standing committees are independent, the general assembly approves by special resolution, and a governance committee oversees the arrangement (Articles 7 and 7 bis).

The Authority's 2025 circular on annual general assemblies summarizes these rules for the meeting season [18].

**Family companies.** Federal Decree-Law No. 37 of 2022 on Family Companies provides for an optional family charter (Article 6) and for family governance bodies such as a family assembly, family council and family office (Article 18). The company's memorandum of association prevails over the charter [19].

**Data protection.** Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the protection of personal data has been in force since January 2, 2022 [20].

## Qatar

**Commercial Companies Law.** Issued by Law No. (11) of 2015 [21] and amended by Law No. (8) of 2021 [22]. The 2021 amendment strengthened governance [22]:

- At least one-third of the board of a public joint stock company must be independent, and the majority non-executive (Article 97 as replaced).
- Listed companies must have an audit committee (new Article 107 bis), and the chair of a listed company may not hold an executive post or sit on board committees (new Article 98 bis).
- Directors and senior management must disclose interests. Transactions at or above 10% of the lower of the company's market value or net assets need prior general assembly approval, and interested parties may not vote (Article 109 as replaced).

**Governance Code for Listed Companies.** QFMA Board Decision No. (5) of 2025 issued a new code. It was published in the Official Gazette on August 17, 2025 and repeals the 2016 code [23]. Companies had one year from publication to comply, extendable by the QFMA chair [23]. Among its requirements, at least three board members should be independent [23].

## Bahrain

**Corporate Management and Governance Charter.** Issued by Decision No. (19) of 2018 and amended by Decision No. (91) of 2022 [24][25]. It applies to joint stock companies under the Commercial Companies Law, on a comply-or-explain basis, except companies licensed by the Central Bank of Bahrain, which follow the Bank's own rules [24]. Among its principles [24][25]:

- At least half of the board are non-executive, including at least three independent members, and the chair should be independent and should not be the chief executive.
- For public companies, the audit committee is drawn from the board, with at least three members, a majority of independent members and an independent chair.
- Officials with a conflict of interest must disclose it and may not attend, debate or vote on the matter.

## Kuwait

**Companies Law.** Law No. 1 of 2016 promulgated the Companies Law [26]. It was amended by Law No. 15 of 2017.

**Module Fifteen: Corporate Governance.** Part of the Executive Bylaws issued by the Capital Markets Authority, updated in 2026, for listed companies and companies licensed by the Authority [27]. At least 20% of board members must be independent, rounded up, and independent members may not exceed half of the board. The majority of the board must be non-executive [27].

## Oman

**Commercial Companies Law.** Issued by Royal Decree 18/2019, published in the Official Gazette on February 17, 2019 [28]. Among its provisions [28]:

- A board of at least five members for public joint stock companies and three for closed ones, up to eleven (Article 179).
- Directors may not have a direct or indirect interest in the company's transactions except under the regulator's rules, and a register of interests is kept (Article 204).
- Directors must notify the company in writing of their interests within five days (Article 205).

**Regulator.** Royal Decree 20/2024 established the **Financial Services Authority**, which took over the Capital Market Authority's assets and staff. Existing regulations remain in force until the new Authority replaces them [29].

## How this connects to the rest of the collection

The national codes turn the international principles described in ISO 37000:2021 and the G20/OECD Principles into binding or comply-or-explain rules. Common themes run through every code in this map: independent directors, separation of the chair and chief executive, audit committees, conflicts of interest and related party transactions. The drafting guide and templates in this collection help an organization write its own documents in line with whichever code applies to it.
