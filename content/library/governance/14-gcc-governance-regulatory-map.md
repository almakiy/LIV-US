Each GCC state has its own company law and, for listed companies, its own governance code issued by the capital market regulator. This map gives the official instruments, whom they apply to and their headline requirements. It is a starting point for reading the official texts, not a substitute for them.

> **How to use this map.** Requirements change. Several instruments below were amended in 2025 and 2026. Before relying on any point, open the official text through the link, check its date and amendments, and take legal advice. Where an official English text is a translation, the Arabic text prevails.

## Saudi Arabia

**Companies Law.** Issued by Royal Decree No. M/132 dated 1/12/1443H [2]. It applies to all companies. The Ministry of Commerce supervises companies other than listed joint stock companies, and the Capital Market Authority (CMA) supervises listed joint stock companies. The CMA's Implementing Regulation of the Companies Law for Listed Joint Stock Companies was most recently amended in 2026 [3].

**Corporate Governance Regulations (CMA).** Issued by CMA Board Resolution 8-16-2017 and amended by Resolution 8-5-2023 to align with the new Companies Law [1]. Headline requirements for listed companies:

- The majority of board members are non-executive directors, and independent directors number at least two or one-third of the board, whichever is greater [1].
- The chair may not hold any executive position in the company at the same time, including managing director, chief executive or general manager [1].
- The audit committee has three to five members, no executive directors, at least one member specialized in finance and accounting, and an independent director as its chair [1].
- The regulations also address conflicts of interest, related party dealings, internal audit, the board report and remuneration disclosure [1].

The CMA's English text states that Arabic is the official language of the regulations [1]. Some provisions are mandatory for the Main Market and guidance for the Parallel Market (Nomu), so check how each article applies to your company.

**Financial institutions.** The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) has issued Key Principles of Governance in Financial Institutions under its supervision [4].

**Occupational safety and health.** The Occupational Safety and Health Management Regulation, issued by ministerial decision under Article 121 of the Labor Law, applies to establishments employing fifty or more full-time workers [5]. In March 2024 the Council of Ministers approved the Kingdom's accession to ILO Convention No. 187 on the promotional framework for occupational safety and health [17].

**Data protection.** The Personal Data Protection Law and its implementing regulations are published by the Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA), which oversees them [14].

## United Arab Emirates

**Regulator.** Federal Decree-Law No. (32) of 2025 replaced the Securities and Commodities Authority with the **Capital Market Authority** as its legal successor, from January 1, 2026. Earlier decisions of the Authority remain in force unless they conflict with the new law [21].

**Commercial Companies Law.** Federal Decree-Law No. (32) of 2021, in force since January 2, 2022, applies to commercial companies, with separate regimes for free zones that have their own company laws [6][19]. For joint stock companies, it sets several board rules [19]:

- A board of three to eleven members, with terms of up to three years (Article 143).
- A director with a conflicting interest must notify the board, have it recorded in the minutes and not vote (Article 150).
- Related party transactions up to 5% of capital need board approval, and above 5% general assembly approval after a valuation (Article 152).
- A joint stock company may not lend to its directors or their close relatives, except for financial institutions supervised by the Central Bank (Article 153).

The Ministry also lists the 2025 amending Decree-Law No. (20) of 2025 [6].

**Private joint stock companies.** Ministerial Decision No. 137/2024 requires at least one-third of the board to be independent members, with a majority of non-executive members [7].

**Public joint stock companies.** The Governance Guide approved by Decision No. (3/Chairman) of 2020 is published by the Authority, on its corporate governance page, as a consolidated text with its amendments of 2021, 2022, 2024 and 2025 [8][18]. Among its requirements [18]:

- At least one-third of board members are independent, and the majority are non-executive (Article 9, clause 5).
- At least one woman sits on the board, and this is disclosed in the annual governance report (Article 9, clause 3).
- The chair may not hold any other executive position. Since Decision No. (24/Chairman) of 2025, effective August 30, 2025, the chair may also be the company manager only if all of these conditions are met: the articles permit it, at least three-quarters of the board and every member of the standing committees are independent, the general assembly approves by special resolution, and a governance committee oversees the arrangement (Articles 7 and 7 bis).

The Authority's 2025 circular on annual general assemblies summarizes these rules for the meeting season [9].

**Family companies.** Federal Decree-Law No. 37 of 2022 on Family Companies provides for an optional family charter (Article 6) and family governance bodies such as a family assembly, family council and family office (Article 18). The company's memorandum of association prevails over the charter [20].

**Data protection.** Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the protection of personal data has been in force since January 2, 2022 [15].

## Qatar

**Governance Code for Listed Companies.** QFMA Board Decision No. (5) of 2025 issued a new code. It was published in the Official Gazette on August 17, 2025 and repeals the 2016 code for companies listed on the Main Market [10]. It refers to the Commercial Companies Law issued by Law No. (11) of 2015, as amended by Law No. (8) of 2021 [10]. Among its requirements, at least three board members should be independent [10]. Companies were given a transition period to comply, so check the current status for your company.

## Bahrain

**Corporate Governance Code.** Issued by the Ministry of Industry and Commerce for Bahraini joint stock companies, on a comply-or-explain basis [11]. Companies licensed by the Central Bank of Bahrain follow the Bank's own rulebook, including its high-level controls module.

## Kuwait

**Module Fifteen: Corporate Governance.** Part of the Executive Bylaws issued by the Capital Markets Authority, updated in 2026 [12]. Among its requirements, at least 20% of board members must be independent, and independent members may not exceed half of the board [12].

## Oman

**Commercial Companies Law.** Royal Decree 18/2019 [13]. The text linked here is an unofficial reproduction. Cite the Official Gazette for binding text. The governance code for public listed companies was issued by the Capital Market Authority. That body was replaced by the Financial Services Authority in 2024. Its current code and status are still being verified for this map (see below).

## Regional good practice

Hawkamah, the Institute for Corporate Governance in Dubai, works on corporate and family business governance in the region [16]. It is not a regulator: its guidance is good practice, not law.

## How this connects to the rest of the collection

The national codes turn the international principles described in ISO 37000:2021 and the G20/OECD Principles into binding or comply-or-explain rules. Common themes run through every code in this map: independent directors, separation of the chair and chief executive, audit committees, conflicts of interest and related party transactions. The drafting guide and templates in this collection help an organization write its own documents in line with whichever code applies to it.
