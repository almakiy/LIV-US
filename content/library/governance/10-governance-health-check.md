This checklist is LIV Editorial's self-assessment tool. It is organized around the principles of ISO 37000:2021 [1] and the components of internal control described by COSO [3]. It is not the ISO 37004:2023 maturity model, which is a separate and more detailed instrument [2]. Use it once a year, or before a major change such as a new owner, a new branch or a loan.

**How to score:** 0 = no, 1 = partly or informally, 2 = yes, written down and followed. The maximum is 60.

## A. Purpose and accountability

1. Is the organization's purpose written down in a few sentences?
2. Do all owners agree on the purpose and on the return they expect?
3. Is it clear who governs (owner, owners' council or board) and how they decide together?
4. Does each owner, director and manager know what they are accountable for?
5. Is there a written owners' or shareholders' agreement where there is more than one owner?

## B. Decision rights

6. Is there a written list of decisions only the owners or the board may take?
7. Is there a delegation of authority with money limits for spending, discounts, refunds and hiring?
8. Do the people named in the delegation have a copy and follow it?
9. Are decisions outside the delegation recorded and reported?
10. Is there a deputy for every critical authority when the holder is absent?

## C. Oversight and control

11. Does the governing body review results against budget at least monthly or quarterly?
12. Is there a register of the main risks with an owner for each, reviewed on a set cycle?
13. Are cash, payments and stock controlled by counts, reconciliations and two-person approvals?
14. Are access rights to systems and bank accounts restricted and reviewed?
15. Does someone independent of management, such as an outside accountant or auditor, review the accounts or controls each year?

## D. Integrity and compliance

16. Is there a written code of conduct that staff have read?
17. Are conflicts of interest declared in writing and recorded before decisions?
18. Is there a gifts and hospitality rule with a register?
19. Is there a calendar of licenses, permits, filings and renewals with a named owner?
20. Can staff raise a concern outside their line manager without fear of retaliation?

## E. Transparency and stakeholders

21. Do all owners receive the same accurate information at the same time?
22. Are transactions with owners, relatives and their companies approved and recorded?
23. Are customer complaints recorded and reviewed for patterns?
24. Are employees told about the rules that apply to them and changes to those rules?
25. Is information that must be disclosed to authorities, lenders or partners delivered on time?

## F. Review and renewal

26. Has the governing body reviewed its own effectiveness in the past year?
27. Are governance documents dated, versioned and reviewed on schedule?
28. Is there a succession plan for the owner-manager or chief executive?
29. After a failure or a near miss, are the governance lessons recorded and acted on?
30. Has the governance framework changed in the past two years to reflect how the organization has changed?

## Reading your score

| Total | Reading |
|---|---|
| 0 to 20 | Governance is mostly informal. Start with sections A and B. |
| 21 to 40 | The basics exist but are not consistent. Find the lowest section and fix it first. |
| 41 to 54 | A sound framework. Focus on independent review and succession. |
| 55 to 60 | Mature for an organization of your size. Consider an external review to confirm it. |

The total matters less than the **weakest section**. A business that scores well on control but 2 out of 10 on decision rights is still exposed.

*LIV Editorial self-assessment tool, for general use. It is not a certification, an audit or a measure of compliance with any law or standard.*
