These definitions are written in plain language by LIV Editorial. They are not the official definitions of the standards cited. Where a term comes from a standard, the standard's own wording prevails. The main references are ISO 37000:2021 [1], ISO 31000:2018 [2], ISO 37301:2021 [3], the IIA Three Lines Model [4] and the G20/OECD Principles [5].

## A to D

- **Accountability:** being answerable for decisions and their results to someone with the right to ask. One of the foundational principles of ISO 37000 [1].
- **Advisory board:** a group of outside experts who advise owners or management without legal decision-making power. Often a first step for small and family businesses.
- **Assurance:** an independent and objective assessment of whether risk management, control and governance work as intended. In the Three Lines Model, independent assurance is the role of internal audit [4].
- **Compliance obligations:** requirements an organization must comply with, and commitments it chooses to comply with [3].
- **Conflict of interest:** a situation in which a personal, family or financial interest could influence, or appear to influence, a decision taken for the organization.
- **Control activities:** the actions that make sure decisions are carried out as intended, such as approvals, reconciliations and segregation of duties.
- **Delegation of authority (DoA):** the written record of who may take which decisions, within which limits.
- **Due diligence:** checks carried out before a decision or relationship, for example on a new supplier or agent.

## E to I

- **Family charter (family constitution):** a written statement of a business family's values, its vision and its policies on employment, ownership, dividends and succession.
- **Governance:** the system by which an organization is directed, overseen and held accountable for achieving its purpose [1].
- **Governing body:** the person or group with ultimate responsibility for the organization's governance, such as a board of directors, a board of trustees or an owner [1].
- **Independent director:** a board member free of relationships with management or controlling owners that could affect their judgment. National codes define independence in detail.
- **Internal control:** the processes an organization uses to give reasonable assurance that its objectives for operations, reporting and compliance are met.

## J to R

- **Management:** the people who run the organization day to day, within the direction and authority set by the governing body.
- **Proportionality:** matching the scale of governance arrangements to the size, complexity and risk of the organization.
- **Related party transaction:** a transaction between the organization and its owners, directors, managers, their relatives or companies they control. The G20/OECD Principles expect such transactions to be approved and conducted in a way that manages conflicts and protects the company and its shareholders [5].
- **Reserved matters:** decisions that only the owners or the governing body may take.
- **Risk:** the effect of uncertainty on objectives [2].
- **Risk appetite:** the amount and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or accept to achieve its objectives.
- **Risk register:** a list of identified risks with their assessment, treatment, owner and review date.

## S to Z

- **Segregation of duties:** dividing a task, such as ordering, receiving and paying, among different people so that no one person controls it from start to finish.
- **Stakeholder:** a person or organization that can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a decision or activity.
- **Three Lines Model:** the IIA's model of roles: the governing body; management with first and second line roles; and internal audit as the independent third line [4].
- **Tone at the top:** the ethical climate set by the governing body and senior leaders through what they say, decide and tolerate.
- **Whistleblowing (speaking up):** reporting suspected wrongdoing through channels designed to protect the person who reports.
