A delegation of authority (DoA) puts the accountability principle of ISO 37000:2021 into daily practice [1]. It shows, decision by decision, who may act on the organization's behalf and within what limits. It also supports the "control activities" component of internal control described by COSO [2]. Copy this template, replace the bracketed items and delete the rows you do not need.

## Key to the matrix

- **P** proposes or prepares
- **C** must be consulted before the decision
- **A** approves (one approver per row and limit)
- **I** is informed afterward
- **Limit:** the maximum amount per transaction, or per year where stated, in [currency]

The G20/OECD Principles expect related party transactions to be approved and conducted in a way that ensures proper management of conflicts of interest and protects the interests of the company and its shareholders [3]. That is why row 16 sits with the board or owners whatever the amount.

## Rules that go with the matrix

1. **Splitting is not allowed.** A purchase may not be divided to stay under a limit.
2. **No self-approval.** Nobody approves a payment to themselves, a decision in which they have a declared conflict, or their own expenses.
3. **Budget first.** A limit applies only to expenditure within the approved budget, unless the row says otherwise.
4. **Absence.** Each approver names a deputy in writing. A deputy approves within the same limits and informs the approver.
5. **Exceptions.** An urgent decision outside the matrix is recorded with the reason and reported to the next level within [number] working days.
6. **Systems follow the matrix.** Payment, purchasing and point-of-sale system limits are set to match the matrix, and they are reviewed when the matrix changes.
7. **Review.** The board or owners review the matrix at least once a year and after any reorganization.
