A conflict of interest exists when a personal, family or financial interest could influence, or could appear to influence, a decision someone takes for the organization. Conflicts are normal, especially in family businesses and small communities. What harms an organization is a conflict that is **hidden** or **not managed**. The G20/OECD Principles expect related party transactions to be handled so that conflicts are properly managed and the company and its shareholders are protected [1]. Anti-bribery management under ISO 37001:2025 also addresses gifts, hospitality and conflicts as routes to improper influence [2]. Both support the accountability expected of governing bodies under ISO 37000:2021 [3].

## Part 1: Declaration form

**Name:** [ ]  **Role:** [ ]  **Date:** [ ]

Do you, or a close family member, have any of the following? Answer each with "yes" or "no". Give details for every "yes".

1. An ownership interest in, or a job, board seat or paid role with, a supplier, customer, competitor, landlord or partner of the organization
2. A loan to or from, or a guarantee involving, any such party
3. A family relationship with another employee, where one of you supervises, pays, evaluates or approves the work of the other
4. A gift, hospitality, trip or favor received from any such party in the past 12 months above [value]
5. Any other interest that a reasonable person might think could influence your decisions

I will update this declaration within [number] days of any change, and in any case once a year.

**Signature:** [ ]

Typical actions agreed:

- **No action needed:** the interest is minor and unrelated to the person's decisions.
- **Step out:** the person takes no part in decisions involving the party, and someone else approves.
- **Extra check:** the decision is reviewed by a second approver or by the board.
- **Remove the conflict:** the interest is given up, or the person changes role.

## Part 4: Rules for meetings and decisions

1. At the start of each board, owners' or management meeting, the chair asks: "Does anyone have an interest in any item on today's agenda?" Declarations are minuted.
2. A person with a declared interest leaves the discussion and the vote on that item, unless the meeting decides otherwise and records why.
3. Transactions with owners, directors, managers, their relatives or their companies are approved by the board or owners, at market terms, and recorded.
4. Cash gifts and gifts during a tender or negotiation are always declined.
5. Breaches of these rules are reported to [role] and handled under the disciplinary procedure.

*Starter template for general use. Company law, listing rules and sector regulation may set specific rules for conflicts of interest and related party transactions, including disclosure and shareholder approval. Check them and take legal advice before adopting this template.*
