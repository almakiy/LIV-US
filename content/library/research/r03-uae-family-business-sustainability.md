**Study:** Oudah, Jabeen and Dixon (2018), *Determinants Linked to Family Business Sustainability in the UAE: An AHP Approach*, Sustainability 10(1), 246 [1].

## The question

Which factors matter most for the long-term survival of medium and large family businesses in the United Arab Emirates [1]?

## Method and sample

The authors used the analytic hierarchy process (AHP), a structured method for ranking criteria by pairwise comparison. Their model had seven criteria and fifteen sub-criteria [1]. Data came from an interview-based survey of twelve medium and large UAE family firms, covering transitions from the first to the second generation and from the second to the third [1].

## Key findings

- **Large family firms plan for the long term.** They were aware of the risk of a failed generational transition and planned accordingly. They gave less weight to family values and family capital [1].
- **Medium family firms focus on the short term.** They were less aware of transition risk and concentrated on short-term returns [1].
- **The gap is governance.** The authors conclude that medium-sized firms need more succession planning, strategic planning and formal corporate governance [1].
- **A practical checklist.** The ranked model gives families and their advisers a list of priorities for continuity [1].

## Limitations

The sample was small (twelve firms) and the model used a limited number of criteria. The findings cannot be generalized to all UAE family businesses. Succession is also a sensitive subject for interviews, which may affect what respondents said [1].

## What it means for practitioners

- A medium-sized family business that has grown fast is often the one most exposed at the next generational transition.
- The family governance institutions described in the IFC Family Business Governance Handbook give a structure for acting on these findings: family assembly, family council and family charter [2].
- Start with a written succession plan and a family employment policy, then strengthen the board.
