**Study:** Ferrazzi and Tueske (2022), *Small and Medium Enterprises in Emerging Economies: The Achilles' Heel of Corporate ESG Responsibility Practices?* World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 10065 [1]. It was prepared as a background paper for World Bank work on sustainable private sector growth in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA).

## The question

How do the environmental, social and governance (ESG) practices of private firms in emerging economies, most of them SMEs, compare by firm size and region, with a focus on MENA [1]?

## Method and sample

The authors built a composite indicator from World Bank Enterprise Survey data collected between 2018 and 2020. The data cover about 28,000 interviews with owners and top managers in 41 economies [1]. Firms were registered businesses with more than five employees in manufacturing, retail and other services [1]. The governance pillar of the indicator draws on practices such as:

- a written strategy with key performance indicators
- having a board of directors or supervisory board
- external audit of the financial statements
- quality certification
- management practices

## Key findings

- **Size matters.** SMEs lag large firms on ESG practices across the emerging economies studied [1].
- **MENA's small firms score lowest** of all regions and size classes in the sample [1].
- **Governance is the relative strength.** Within MENA, the governance pillar scores better than the environmental and social pillars [1].
- **Disclosure and support.** The authors call for ESG and governance disclosure by SMEs on a par with financial reporting, supported by banks and governments [1].

## Limitations

- The Enterprise Survey was not designed to measure ESG, and the indicator does not match any ESG reporting standard [1].
- The data were collected before the Covid-19 pandemic [1].
- The MENA economies in the sample do not include the GCC states [1].
- It is a working paper and has not been through journal peer review.

## What it means for practitioners

- The basics of governance in the indicator are within reach of any small business: a written strategy with a few indicators, an external review of the accounts and documented management practices. Banks and investors increasingly look for them.
- For GCC readers, the findings are context, not direct evidence, because no GCC economy was in the sample.
- ISO 37000:2021 presents governance as relevant to organizations of every size [2].
