The three lines model describes how governing bodies, management and internal audit share the work of managing risk and providing assurance. The Institute of Internal Auditors updated it in 2020 as the Three Lines Model [3]. Both studies below used the earlier "three lines of defense" framing.

## The studies

| Study | Setting and sample | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Tawfik, Durrah and Aljawhar (2023) [1] | Omani industrial companies. 159 valid questionnaires from internal auditors, finance managers, general managers, board members and audit committee members | Structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) |
| Bantleon et al. (2021) [2] | 415 chief audit executives in Germany, Austria and Switzerland | Survey with logistic regressions |

## Key findings

**Oman** [1]:

- All three lines had significant positive effects on governance. The three lines were management's commitment to legal, regulatory and ethical requirements; risk, compliance and quality functions; and assurance-focused internal audit.
- Senior management and the board act as stewards of the model rather than as extra lines.
- The model can be used to diagnose gaps in control and compliance.

**Germany, Austria and Switzerland** [2]:

- About 90% of organizations had implemented the model, and 45% reported no coordination problems.
- There were fewer problems in listed firms, where internal audit has good relations with executives and the supervisory board, and where audit focuses on assurance.
- Financial-sector firms faced more coordination problems, especially with compliance, regulators and external auditors.
- The size of the internal audit function did not matter.

## Limitations

- The Omani study is perception-based, covers one sector in one country and did not survey external stakeholders [1].
- The European study covers German-speaking countries only, and its data are self-reported by chief audit executives [2].
- Neither study measures outcomes such as losses or fraud directly.

## What it means for practitioners

- The model helps most when roles are clear and the lines talk to each other. Map who does what in risk, compliance, quality and HSE, and agree how findings are shared.
- Keep internal audit focused on assurance and independent from management, as the 2020 model emphasizes [3].
- Use it together with a risk management framework such as ISO 31000:2018, so that each line works from the same risk picture [4].
- For GCC organizations, the Omani findings are directly relevant regional evidence, although from a single sector.
