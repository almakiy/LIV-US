**Study:** Walters, Wadsworth, Hasle, Refslund and Ramioul (2018), *Safety and health in micro and small enterprises in the EU: Final report from the 3-year SESAME project*, European Agency for Safety and Health at Work (EU-OSHA) [1].

## The question

What works to improve occupational safety and health (OSH) in micro and small enterprises, for whom, and in what circumstances [1]?

## Method and sample

The project combined four sources [1]:

- a literature review
- secondary analysis of EU-OSHA's European enterprise survey (ESENER-2)
- 162 workplace case studies with owner-managers and workers in nine EU member states
- 44 good-practice cases from twelve countries

Workshops with intermediaries, such as employers' organizations, trade unions and sector associations, complemented the research.

## Key findings

- **Survival comes first.** Many micro and small enterprises follow "low road" survival strategies. Scarce resources and knowledge, and weak worker protection, help explain their higher OSH risks [1].
- **Owner-managers differ.** The report distinguishes owner-managers who avoid OSH, those who react to problems and those who learn. Support must be tailored to each and easy to adopt [1].
- **Regulation and intermediaries matter.** Sustained regulation and inspection matter, and so do intermediaries who reach small firms where authorities cannot [1].
- **Workers' voices are missing.** The workers' perspective is largely absent from research on small-firm safety [1].

## Limitations

The study covers the EU only. The case studies are qualitative, and the data were collected between 2014 and 2017 [1].

## What it means for practitioners

- For shops, workshops and small branches, OSH works best when it is part of the owner's routine: a short monthly walk-round, simple checklists and involving the people who do the work.
- Sector associations, chambers of commerce, suppliers and insurers can be powerful channels for practical safety support to small businesses.
- The three essential principles in the UK Health and Safety Executive's leadership guidance are written for business owners as well as boards: leadership, worker involvement, and assessment and review [2].
