**Study:** Li, Tang, Yang and Zhang (2026), *Impact of leadership styles on safety performance in risky workplaces: a meta-analysis*, Frontiers in Psychology 17, 1848283 [1].

## The question

How do 21 different leadership styles relate to safety performance, and under what conditions do the effects change [1]?

## Method and sample

A meta-analysis of 102 studies with 107,109 participants in 24 countries, published from 1999 to 2024 [1]. Most studies came from medium- and high-risk sectors such as mining, construction, chemicals, nuclear power and aviation [1].

## Key findings

- **Most styles help, some harm.** Thirteen styles were positively associated with safety performance and four negatively. The negative ones include abusive, destructive and passive leadership [1].
- **Transformational leadership contributes most.** Leaders who inspire, set an example and develop their people had the strongest positive association [1].
- **Context matters.** Effects varied with national culture, power distance, research method and risk level. Transformational leadership had a stronger effect in developed countries, and some styles showed a U-shaped pattern across risk levels [1].
- **Two routes to safety.** Monitoring and control styles mainly drove compliance with rules. Motivational and supportive styles drove voluntary participation in safety. The authors conclude that a combination of styles works best [1].

## Limitations

Culture and risk were measured with country-level proxies. All data were self-reported. Some styles were represented by fewer than six studies, so their results are less certain [1].

## What it means for practitioners

- Leadership development for supervisors and managers should build both sets of behaviors: clear rules and follow-up, and support that invites workers to speak up and take part.
- Abusive or passive supervision is a safety risk in itself and belongs in the risk register, not only in HR files.
- Leadership and worker participation are already central requirements of ISO 45001:2018, which devotes its clause 5 to them [2].
