**Study:** Singh et al. (2026), *The role of leadership and governance in occupational safety and health: a rapid evidence assessment for international standard development*, a technical report from the Lloyd's Register Foundation and RAND Europe [1].

## The question

How do senior managers and boards influence occupational safety and health (OSH) outcomes? Which competencies matter, and which contextual factors shape the effect [1]?

## Method and sample

A rapid evidence assessment: a structured but time-limited review. It searched five databases for studies published from 2015 to 2025, in six languages [1]. Of 1,061 records screened, 80 studies were included. The largest share came from the United States, and the studies include research from Saudi Arabia [1].

## Key findings

Senior leaders improve OSH through four main routes [1]:

1. **Strategic direction:** an OSH policy, objectives, and safety built into business strategy
2. **Visible leadership:** site visits and taking part in safety meetings
3. **Systematic oversight:** indicators, reviews, investigations and incentives
4. **Cultural leadership:** a "just culture" in which people feel safe reporting problems

The review also found the following [1]:

- Board diversity and strong chief executives were associated with better OSH outcomes, provided directors had real authority and accountability.
- Leaders who were overconfident, disengaged or focused on production at all costs were associated with more incidents.

## Limitations

- Most of the evidence is correlational, so it shows associations rather than proven causes [1].
- Standards and studies define "senior management" in different ways [1].
- Small enterprises are under-represented, in part because "founder" was not a search term [1].
- There was no full quality appraisal of each study, and only academic literature was included [1].

## What it means for practitioners

- The four routes give a governing body a simple structure for its own HSE agenda: set direction, be visible, oversee systematically, and build a reporting culture.
- They are consistent with the leadership and worker participation requirements of ISO 45001:2018 [2]. They also match the three essential principles in the UK Health and Safety Executive's guidance for directors [3].
- Owners of small businesses should treat the findings as directional: the evidence comes mostly from larger organizations.
