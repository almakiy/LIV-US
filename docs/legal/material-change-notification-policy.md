## 1. Purpose
LIV needs accurate information about capability and delivery within each authorized scope. A change notice supports review; it does not itself approve a new activity, scope or site.

## 2. Materiality and timing
P-12/MC01 must establish the binding categories, significance tests, prior-approval events and notification deadline. Existing form categories are placeholders. Examples discussed in project documents include changes to trainers, ownership, sites and platforms; these examples are not a complete approved legal definition.

## 3. Notice content
Use the partner oversight route, identifying the provider, affected scope, actual or proposed change, date, reasons, supporting evidence, learner impact and temporary controls. Keep the original facts and any subsequent updates. Do not backdate a notice to imply prior approval.

## 4. Review
LIV records and assigns the notice. A material-change review may require additional evidence or a special review and can refer a scope/title expansion to its proper approval route. A different responsible decision role must take any consequential authorization decision where separation is required.

## 5. Pending decision
Operate only within currently authorized limits. MC01 must establish whether each type of proposed change requires approval before use and the treatment of urgent unavoidable changes. A notice, fee or silence does not establish authorization.

## 6. Outcomes and records
Record reasons, conditions, effective dates and scope amendments. A change review does not automatically invalidate historical credentials. Misleading non-disclosure may be investigated under the agreement, with the response and appeal routes preserved.
