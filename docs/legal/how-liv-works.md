## 1. What LIV is
LIV is a private U.S.-based organization. It defines credential schemes, authorizes training providers to deliver training within an approved scope, issues the credential record, and keeps a public record that anyone can check. LIV is not a government agency or a regulator, and it does not claim any government, federal, ISO or other external accreditation or recognition.

## 2. What you can rely on
- **A credential record is checked online.** Every valid credential can be checked with its number or QR code. A revoked credential shows as revoked.
- **A provider's authorization is public.** The public register shows each provider's status, its approved scope and its level. Status is shown only after LIV approves it.
- **Our rules are published.** Each rule has a version and an effective date. Changes do not affect records already issued.
- **Records are not rewritten.** An issued credential is not changed. A mistake is corrected by a logged step, and the original record remains on file.

## 3. What LIV does not do
- LIV does not deliver training itself under the Authorized Training Provider program. Providers deliver it.
- LIV does not sell a credential, a result or a place on a course.
- A payment or a membership does not give a credential, does not give an assessment result, and does not change an application.
- A training completion record is not a professional qualification or certification, and it is not a CPD record.
- LIV does not guarantee employment, pay or licensing outcomes.

## 4. How a provider becomes authorized
1. The provider applies with its organization details, its scope and its courses.
2. LIV checks the application against the published criteria. The team that checks the file is separate from the person who decides.
3. A decision is recorded with reasons. A provider may appeal under the Complaints and Appeals Policy.
4. Authorization is confirmed only after the checks needed to activate it are complete.
5. The provider keeps to its scope. Courses outside the scope are not listed.

Full criteria: see the ATP Program Rules and the Course Quality Standard.

## 5. How a course is listed
A course must meet the Course Quality Standard: clear objectives, a timed agenda, qualified trainers, policies, a way to check learning, a review cycle and feedback. LIV checks each course before listing it. A course that is missing information is returned with the gaps named.

## 6. How impartiality works
Fees pay for described work. They never buy a better decision. Membership, a gift, a partnership level or a commercial relationship gives no advantage in any decision. Staff who review a matter do not decide it. Staff declare conflicts of interest every year. See the Impartiality and Conflicts of Interest Statement and the Governance Structure.

## 7. How partner levels work
Every provider starts at Bronze. Promotion depends on a record LIV has observed over time: months of operation, a clean record and passed reviews. No provider can buy a higher level, and a higher level gives no exemption from review. See the Partner Levels page.

## 8. How requests for training are shared
When an organization asks LIV for a quote, LIV sends the request at the same time to up to five authorized providers with that scope, choosing those that received the fewest requests recently. Level does not give priority. LIV does not set the price and is not a party to the contract between the organization and the provider.

## 9. How complaints are handled
Anyone may complain about a provider, a course, a credential or LIV. A complaint is acknowledged, investigated by someone not involved in the matter, and answered in writing. If you disagree with the answer, you may appeal. See the Complaints and Appeals Policy and the Learner Rights Charter.

## 10. How your information is used
We collect only what we need for the service you asked for. Marketing messages are separate and need your consent. You may download your data and ask us to close your account. See the Privacy Policy.

## 11. Digital badges
Each valid credential and each active membership can have a digital badge. A badge is a signed record that others can check against LIV's public key. A badge stops working when the credential is revoked or the membership ends. A membership badge says membership only.

## 12. Where to ask
Questions about this guide, a provider or a record go to the contact page. We answer in writing and give a date for our reply.
