# ATP partnership levels: Bronze, Silver, Gold (draft for owner approval)

Status (October 18, 2026): **proposal.** The owner approved the idea and the names on October 18: **Bronze** (base), **Silver**, **Gold**. Every number below (months, counts) is a proposed starting value awaiting approval; none is applied. Nothing in this document changes the authorization criteria (`PARTNER-AUTHORIZATION.md` §3), which are the same for every level. Code and screens are built later (Batch H); until then levels are contract terms. Price effects are in `FEES-REVIEW.md` §9.

## 1. What a level is, and is not
- A level is a **commercial partnership level**: the size of the commitment, the scope and a record with LIV. It is not a rating of training quality, and every provider meets the same criteria and is under the same surveillance rules, whatever its level.
- Billing plan and level are the same thing: **Bronze = Starter plan, Silver = Professional, Gold = Enterprise.** Plan codes in the price catalog keep their internal names.
- **Never on the credential.** Learners' certificates, the verification page and the public register's provider facts are identical for every level. The badge appears on the provider's own marketing and profile page, with the sentence: *"Partnership levels reflect commitment and track record with LIV, not the quality of training. Every LIV Authorized Training Provider meets the same authorization criteria."*
- Licence of the badge: use only as supplied; withdrawn the day authorization is suspended or the level is lost.

## 2. Entry: everyone starts at Bronze
No new provider enters at Silver or Gold, however large and whatever it offers to pay. Reasons: the criteria are the same for all; a level rests on a record LIV has observed; and one entry rule is the simplest to defend as impartial.

## 3. Promotion criteria (all must be met)

| # | Criterion | Bronze to Silver | Silver to Gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Minimum time | **12 months** as Bronze | **12 months** as Silver and **24 months** in total |
| 2 | Surveillance | One completed annual surveillance review with no major finding | Two consecutive clean surveillance reviews |
| 3 | Sample reviews of batches against training evidence | **2** passed | **4** passed in total |
| 4 | Nonconformities | None major open; every minor closed by its due date | Same, and none major in the last 24 months |
| 5 | Good standing | No suspension in the last 12 months | No suspension in the last 24 months |
| 6 | Complaints | No substantiated complaint unresolved; resolved ones closed within the target | Same |
| 7 | Records | Trainer records current for every scope; two-factor sign-in on for all administrators; learner-protection documents current | Same |
| 8 | Commitment | Annual plan of the new level **paid in advance** | Same |
| 9 | Additional obligations | none | A named quality officer; an annual data return (batches, completions, complaints); capability evidence for the wider footprint (several sites or countries, or very high volume) |

Notes:
- **Issued volume is not a criterion.** Counting certificates would reward issuing more. The commitment is the prepaid plan, and the sample reviews test the truth of the batches.
- **Shortened route to Silver (evidence, not payment):** a provider with a verifiable prior record (the same training delivered for at least three years, documented) may ask for review at **6 months** instead of 12. It must pass the 90-day early sample review and a full surveillance moved to month 6 (the one surveillance the annual fee already includes), plus criteria 3 to 8. There is no shortened route to Gold.

## 4. Can a provider pay more to go faster? No
- No fee, premium or plan shortens a minimum period or lightens a criterion. The only way to a shorter period is the evidence route above, decided on documents.
- A provider may pay a higher plan early only after eligibility is approved; money never creates eligibility.
- Target time for a level decision is published and the same for everyone (proposed: 30 days from a complete request). No paid fast track.
- Why: a purchasable level is a purchased status, which would make the badge untrue to the partner's customers and would be the first thing an impartiality reviewer finds.

## 5. Governance of level decisions
1. **Request:** the provider asks once the minimum period has passed, or LIV proposes it at the annual review. One review a year is included in the annual fee; an extra review in the same year costs **$150** (the fee pays for work, never for the outcome).
2. **Assessment:** the Application Assessor records a finding against each criterion with evidence, using the same assessment, recommendation and input-fingerprint rules as applications (`assessment.js`). A level criteria version is published with an effective date and transition rules, as for authorization criteria.
3. **Decision:** the Authorization Decision-Maker, a different person from the assessor, decides: promote, not yet (with the unmet criteria and a date to ask again), or decline. Reasons are recorded and locked.
4. **Declared interests:** assessors and decision-makers declare interests as for applications; any referral agent has no role.
5. **Effect and billing:** a promotion takes effect on the first day of the next month. The difference between the annual fees is invoiced pro rata for the remaining months, and the certificate allowance rises pro rata. A downgrade takes effect only at renewal.
6. **Appeal:** through the existing appeals procedure (fee as in `FEES.md`, refunded if upheld).
7. **Reporting:** once a year LIV publishes an aggregate, unnamed report: partners and promotions by level, nonconformities and complaints by level, batch anomalies by level. If a level shows worse compliance, criteria are revised.

## 6. Keeping or losing a level
- Reviewed once a year at renewal, never mid-year except by suspension.
- A major nonconformity **freezes** promotion until it is closed; the badge stays.
- Authorization suspended: badge and level privileges withdrawn at once; they return only with reinstatement.
- Two consecutive failed surveillance reviews, or failure to renew the prepaid commitment: moved down one level at the next renewal, after a **90-day** period to correct.
- A downgrade is a recorded, reasoned decision, appealable like a promotion refusal.

## 7. Surveillance is not level-based
Risk (volume jumps, anomalies, complaints, material changes) sets the intensity of review, never the level. A Gold provider is not examined more lightly.
