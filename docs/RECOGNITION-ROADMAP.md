# LIV — Recognition and accreditation roadmap (later phases)

Status: plan only (October 5, 2026). Owner direction: build the PMI-style credential model (`CREDENTIAL-MODEL.md`) in **later** phases, and pursue **CPD** recognition and **ISO/IEC 17024** accreditation to strengthen LIV's standing.

**Rule for every public page and certificate:** LIV may state a recognition only after it has been granted and only in the words the granting body allows. Until then LIV is described as a private United States accreditation organization (see legal guardrails in `ROADMAP.md`). Details below reflect general public knowledge and must be confirmed with each body and a qualified consultant before any claim or application.

## Targets
| Target | What it is | Why it helps LIV |
|---|---|---|
| **CPD recognition** (for example the CPD Standards Office or CPD Certification Service in the UK; in the US, IACET authorized-provider status under ANSI/IACET 1) | Independent recognition that learning activities or the provider meet continuing-education/CPD standards | Lets partners and candidates claim recognised CPD/CEU hours for LIV-aligned courses; widely understood by employers and professional bodies |
| **ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation** (granted by an accreditation body that is an IAF MLA signatory; in the US, ANAB) | International standard for bodies that certify persons; accreditation proves LIV's exam-based credential is run impartially, with valid exams and controlled processes | Credibility of the LIV credential equivalent to recognised personnel-certification schemes; internationally portable |
| **Assessment-based certificate programs** (US: ANAB accreditation under ANSI/ASTM E2659, to be confirmed) | Accreditation for certificate programs that include assessment of learning outcomes | A realistic earlier step than 17024 for the course-level certificates |

Not covered: LIV accrediting training providers is not itself an ISO/IEC 17024 activity. Whether any accreditation applies to LIV's partner-accreditation scheme must be asked of ANAB or a consultant; do not claim it.

## Who can recognise LIV? (landscape, answer to the owner's question of October 5, 2026)
General public knowledge; confirm each item with the body concerned and a qualified consultant before any application or claim.

**Key fact:** there is **no single government or international body that accredits a private organization in LIV's exact role** (an accreditor of training providers that also issues curricula and credentials). In the US, accreditation of non-degree training providers is voluntary and not government-regulated; the formal international system (ISO/IEC 17011, IAF, ILAC) accredits *conformity assessment bodies* (certification bodies, labs, inspection bodies), not training-provider accreditors. So LIV's standing is built by recognition of its **programs and credentials**, certification of **LIV itself**, and reputation, not by one "accreditor of accreditors".

| Route | Who | What it recognises | Fit for LIV |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accreditation of the credential scheme** (persons) | ANAB under ISO/IEC 17024; NCCA (the accreditation arm of the Institute for Credentialing Excellence, ICE) under its own standards | The exam-based LIV credential is run impartially and validly | **Best long-term target** (Phase 9 onward) |
| **Accreditation of certificate programs** | ANAB (assessment-based certificate programs, ANSI/ASTM E2659, to be confirmed) | Course-level certificates with assessed learning outcomes | Realistic earlier step |
| **Continuing-education provider status** | IACET (ANSI/IACET 1 standard; Authorized/Accredited Provider) | LIV or its partners may award CEUs | Good for partners' course value; fits US identity |
| **CPD recognition** | CPD Standards Office or CPD Certification Service (UK, widely used internationally) | Courses/providers meet CPD standards | Good and relatively quick for selected courses |
| **Management-system certification of LIV itself** | An accredited certification body, for **ISO 9001** (quality), **ISO 21001** (educational organizations), **ISO 29993** (learning services outside formal education) | LIV's own processes are certified (this is certification, not accreditation) | **Achievable early**; allows an honest "ISO-certified management system" statement in the exact words the certifier allows |
| **Institutional accreditation of training institutions** | ACCET and similar (recognised by the US Department of Education) | Institutions that *deliver* training | Not LIV's role (LIV does not deliver training); relevant to partners |
| **Professional-body endorsement or alignment** | Subject bodies (safety, quality, project management associations) | Alignment or approval of curricula | By agreement only; never claim without a written agreement |
| **State authorization** | Virginia (SCHEV scope) | Rules for delivering education and awarding degrees | Keep LIV out of delivery and degrees; confirm with counsel |

**What this means in practice**
1. Do not describe LIV as "accredited by" any body until granted. Memberships and listings are not accreditation.
2. Fastest honest credibility gains: ISO 9001 (then ISO 21001) certification of LIV, CPD recognition of selected courses, IACET provider status.
3. Longest-lasting credibility: ANAB (ISO/IEC 17024) or NCCA accreditation of the LIV credential, after an operating record exists.
4. Partner accreditation by LIV stays a private, published scheme: strong written criteria, audits, public register, impartiality and appeals. It earns trust through transparency and the recognitions above.

## What the credential scheme must be able to show an assessor
Impartiality (committee, risk register, conflict-of-interest declarations), job-task analysis and a published exam content outline, psychometrically sound exam development and item-bank security, candidate handbook and rules, appeals and complaints procedure, records and retention, management system documents and internal audits, management review, surveillance of approved partners, and a track record of operation (assessors typically expect a period of live operation before accreditation).

## Engineering readiness we can build in now (cheap now, expensive later)
- Immutable **audit logs** and **version history** for content, partner decisions and credential decisions (content versions exist now).
- **Named human sign-off** on content and, later, on credential decisions (exists for content).
- **Records retention** policy and export tools.
- **Complaints and appeals** module (cases, deadlines, independent reviewer).
- **Partner surveillance**: periodic reviews, findings and corrective actions recorded per partner.
- **Document control** for LIV's own standards, handbooks and policies (versioned, approved, dated).

## Suggested sequence
1. Now to Phase 8: build the knowledge hub, content engines, course catalogue and candidate accounts with the readiness items above.
2. Hire or appoint: accreditation consultant, impartiality committee/governance board, subject-matter reviewers, psychometrician for exam design.
3. Operate the exam scheme for a period; collect records.
4. Apply first for CPD/IACET recognition of selected courses and for assessment-based certificate program accreditation.
5. Apply for ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation of the credential scheme once the operating record and documentation are in place.
6. After each grant, update site wording and certificates (for example an accreditation mark) exactly as permitted; revisit the "verified" badge idea then.
