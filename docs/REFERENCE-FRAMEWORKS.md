# Ready-made governance frameworks: catalog, reuse rights and LIV's first layer

Status (October 10, 2026): catalog prepared at the owner's request. Before LIV writes its own standards (Track S), it uses ready-made frameworks from official and international bodies as a **first layer**:
- Where the license allows, LIV quotes, republishes and adapts them.
- Elsewhere, LIV learns from how they are organized and summarizes them in its own words.

The first-layer list and the drafting pattern are decision D-S.6 in `BUILD-PLAN.md`.

**Evidence.** Four verification passes on October 10, 2026 read each framework's current edition and its reuse terms on the issuer's own pages or PDFs. Copies, with SHA-256 hashes, are kept outside the repository for the reviewer. Sites that refused automated readers were not bypassed. What no official copy confirmed is marked **not verified**.

## 1. Reuse classes
| Class | Meaning | What LIV may do |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | U.S. federal work (17 U.S.C. §105), UK Open Government Licence v3.0, CC BY, or EUR-Lex reuse policy | Quote, republish and adapt, including in paid products, with the required attribution |
| **B** | Open with limits: unaltered copies only, non-commercial only, or short excerpts only | Cite, quote within the limit, summarize in LIV's words. No modified or paid version without written permission |
| **C** | Free to read, all rights reserved | Summarize in LIV's words, cite, short quotations only. Ask permission for anything more |
| **D** | Paid or member-only | Cite by number and clause, summarize in LIV's words |

Ideas, structures and methods are not protected by copyright; only the wording and graphics are. LIV may therefore design its standards on the pattern of any framework below, in any class, as long as it uses its own words.

Common conditions:
1. **Attribution in the form the issuer asks for.** For example, "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0" or "Reprinted courtesy of the National Institute of Standards and Technology".
2. **No issuer logos.**
3. **No wording that suggests the issuer's approval or official status.** OGL, NIST, COSO, IFC, IoDSA (King), GRI, 1EdTech and others state this. A correct attribution line is enough; no denial statement is needed.
4. **No AI processing where it is forbidden.** ICC, GRI, IFRS and UNESCO terms, and the terms of Singapore's AI Verify Foundation, forbid text and data mining, AI training or automated copying. Their texts are never loaded into LIV's AI engines. A person summarizes them.

Attribution lines:
- GAO, OMB, USSC, DOJ and OSHA: cite the document. These are U.S. Government works.
- NIST: "Reprinted courtesy of the National Institute of Standards and Technology, U.S. Department of Commerce." NIST grants a worldwide right to reprint and make derivative works.
- UK texts: "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."
- EUR-Lex: cite the source, do not distort the meaning, and note that only the Official Journal is authentic.

## 4. The drafting pattern for LIV standards
These patterns come from the frameworks above. Ideas and structure are free to use. The text of GovS 001 and its handbook is also open (OGL), so it can be adapted directly.

1. **Fixed sections** (after GovS 001):
   1. About this standard: purpose, scope, references.
   2. Principles.
   3. Context.
   4. Governance: roles, accountability, assurance.
   5. The subject clauses.

   Annex A lists references; Annex B is the glossary.
2. **Verb table on the first page:**
   - "shall" = requirement;
   - "should" = recommendation, met or explained;
   - "may" = permission;
   - "can" = possibility;
   - "is" = description.

   Legal duties are reported as the law states them and are never invented. Every clause is numbered, and no shall or should appears in notes or annexes.
3. **Layers inside each subject:** outcome → principle → requirements ("shall") → recommended practices ("should") → examples of evidence. This combines the King V outcomes, the Charity Code evidence lists and the Green Book documentation requirements.
4. **Scaling by size:**
   - **Three organization tiers:**
     1. micro business and shop;
     2. small, medium and family business;
     3. large, listed and state-owned.

     Each tier names the clauses that apply to it, after the pattern of the ecoDa phases, the Sport Code tiers and the MCCG large-company practices.
   - **Four maturity levels** for self-assessment: developing, good, better, best. The pattern comes from the IFC matrices and the GovS continuous improvement framework; "good" is the expected minimum.
5. **Disclosure:** "apply and explain". An organization states how it applies each principle.
6. **Document control:**
   - Code `LIV-STD-<nnn>`.
   - Version 1.0 / 1.1.
   - Status label on every page: Draft, For public comment, For approval, Approved, Superseded.
   - Change note.
   - Review at least every 2 years, with revision or reaffirmation at least every 5 years.
   - Public comment and a committee vote under the procedure in Track S (S.1).
7. **Attribution block** on the copyright page: each Class A source adapted, with its license line.
