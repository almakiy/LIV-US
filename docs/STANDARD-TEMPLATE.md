# LIV standard template

Status: template approved with decision D-S.6 (October 12, 2026). Every LIV standard starts from this file.

The pattern follows the drafting conventions in `REFERENCE-FRAMEWORKS.md` §4:
- the fixed sections and verb table of the UK Government Functional Standards (GovS 001, Open Government Licence v3.0);
- the outcome → principle → practice layering seen in King V and the Charity Governance Code;
- the documentation requirements of the GAO Green Book;
- scaling by size and maturity, after the ecoDa phases, the Sport Code tiers, the IFC matrices and the GovS improvement framework.

The wording below is LIV's own.

A standard is drafted and approved only through the standards-development procedure (Track S, S.1), which covers a committee, public comment and approval. No standard is published by an engine, and the HSE Governance credential scheme is not defined by any standard until its scheme governance approves it.


## Using this standard
| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| shall | A requirement of this standard |
| should | A recommendation. An organization meets it or explains what it does instead and why |
| may | A permission |
| can | A possibility or capability |
| is, are | A description |

Where a law or regulation applies, the standard reports it as the law states it and cites the official text. This standard does not add legal duties.

Clauses are numbered. Notes and annexes contain no "shall" or "should".

## 1. About this standard
1.1 **Purpose.** One or two sentences on the outcome the standard serves.

1.2 **Scope.** Who and what it covers, and what it leaves to other standards.

1.3 **Organization tiers.** Each clause names the tiers it applies to:
- **T1:** micro business or shop.
- **T2:** small, medium or family business.
- **T3:** large, listed or state-owned organization.

1.4 **References.** Standards and frameworks used (Annex A).

## 2. Principles
2.1 to 2.n. Short statements, each one sentence, that hold for every tier.

## 3. Context
How the subject relates to the organization's purpose, risks and stakeholders. Descriptive text only.

## 4. Governance
4.1 **Accountability.** Who is accountable, to whom, for what. Roles are described by function, not job title.

4.2 **Oversight and reporting.** What the governing body receives, and how often.

4.3 **Assurance.** How the organization knows the arrangements work, and who provides that assurance.

## 5 onward. Subject clauses
Each subject uses the same layout:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Outcome | What good looks like when the subject is working |
| Principle | The principle from section 2 that this subject applies |
| Requirements | Numbered "shall" clauses, each marked T1, T2 or T3 |
| Recommended practices | Numbered "should" clauses |
| Examples of evidence | Documents or records that show the outcome, such as minutes, a register, a policy or a report. Each documentation requirement is marked **[record]** |
| Scaling note | How a smaller organization meets the outcome with less formality |

## Applying and explaining
An organization that uses this standard publishes a short statement. For each principle, it says how it applies the principle. For each recommendation it does not follow, it says what it does instead.

## Maturity self-assessment (informative)
| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Developing | Some practices exist; not yet consistent or recorded |
| Good | Requirements met and recorded; the expected minimum |
| Better | Recommended practices followed and reviewed regularly |
| Best | Practices improved from evidence and shared with others |

## Annex A. References
Each reference gives its title, edition, issuer and link. Each adapted source carries its attribution line, for example: "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."

## Annex B. Glossary
Terms used in the standard, aligned with the LIV governance glossary.

## Change note
"Version x.y replaces version x.z and has the same purpose and scope", or a list of what changed.

The standard is reviewed at least every 2 years, and revised or reaffirmed at least every 5 years.
